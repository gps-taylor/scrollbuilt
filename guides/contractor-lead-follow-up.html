How fast should a contractor follow up on a new lead?

Faster than the next contractor they contacted. Here's a schedule you can actually keep from a job site.

Updated December 2026 · 6 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

Reply the moment the lead comes in, call the same day, and follow up four or five times over two weeks before closing it out. Automate the first reply so speed doesn't depend on whether you're holding a shovel.

Why speed wins

A homeowner planning a big project usually contacts more than one company. The first one to reply sets the bar: they look organized, and they get the first conversation. Waiting until the weekend to call back hands that to someone else.

A follow-up schedule that works from a job site

  1. Within seconds: an automatic reply confirming you got their request and when they'll hear from a person.
  2. Same day: a call. If they don't pick up, leave a short voicemail and send a text or email saying you called.
  3. Next morning: if you still haven't talked, a short message with two times you're free.
  4. Day 3: one more try, with something useful: a link to your process page or a project like theirs.
  5. Day 7: a check-in: "Still thinking about the backyard? Happy to answer questions."
  6. Day 14: a polite close-out: "I'll close this out for now. If timing changes, reply here and I'll pick it back up."

The close-out message often gets a reply. People who meant to answer get a nudge, and people who went another way usually say so.

What to say in the first reply

Thanks for reaching out to [Company] about your [project]. We got your request. [Name] will call you today from [number]. If there's a better time, reply with it here.

Keep it short, name a person, say when. Don't send a brochure.

Calls you miss

On a job site, calls slip through. A quick text back ("Sorry we missed you, this is [Company]. How can we help?") turns a missed call into a conversation. Only text people who contacted you, and keep it to the job they asked about.

After the site visit

The quote is where most jobs go quiet. Send it within a day or two of the visit, and follow up on it with the same steady rhythm. Our proposal follow-up guide has the timing and the words.

Keep it in one place

Leads arrive by form, phone, text, email and social messages. Put them in one list with a next step and a date on each. A spreadsheet works. A simple inbox with reminders works better, because it nags you.

Questions

Common questions answered.

How many times should I follow up with a lead?

Four or five times over about two weeks, then send a polite close-out message. Fewer than that and you're leaving jobs on the table; many more starts to feel like pressure.

Is it OK to text a lead?

Yes, when they contacted you and gave you their number for that job. Keep messages about their project. Don't add them to marketing texts without clear permission.

What if a lead comes in at night or on a weekend?

Send an automatic reply right away saying when a person will be in touch, then call at the start of the next working day.
