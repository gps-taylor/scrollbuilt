Why doesn't my contractor website get leads?

Usually it's one of nine things. Most take an afternoon to fix.

Updated December 2026 · 7 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

The usual causes: people can't find it, it's slow on phones, it looks older than your work, it doesn't say where you work, it's hard to contact you, it shows no proof, it doesn't explain what happens next, nobody replies fast, or the wrong people are landing on it.

1. Nobody can find it

If your site doesn't name your trade and cities in plain text, search engines can't match it to "pool builder in Scottsdale". Fix: put your trade and service area in the page title and first lines, and complete your Google Business Profile.

2. It's slow on phones

Google has reported that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned if a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load. Fix: test it in Google's PageSpeed Insights and shrink oversized photos first. See our speed guide.

3. It looks older than your work

In the Stanford Web Credibility Project, 46.1% of people mentioned a site's design when judging a company's credibility. Homeowners decide fast: Nielsen Norman Group found the first 10 seconds decide whether a visitor stays. Fix: lead with your best finished work, big, at the top.

4. It doesn't say where you work

A homeowner in a suburb wants to know you'll come to them. Fix: list the cities and areas you serve, on the home page and in your footer.

5. It's hard to reach you

A phone number that isn't tappable, a 12-field form, a contact page three clicks deep. Fix: a tappable phone number and a short form (name, phone, project, city) on every page.

6. It shows no proof

Stock photos of handshakes don't sell a $60,000 project. Fix: real project photos with the city and scope, and reviews near your contact button.

7. It doesn't explain what happens next

Big projects feel risky. Fix: a simple process section, from first call to finished job, with rough timelines.

8. Leads come in, but nobody replies fast

Sometimes the website works and the follow-up doesn't. Fix: an instant automatic reply and a same-day call. Our follow-up guide has a schedule.

9. The wrong people are landing on it

Ads that send everyone to your home page, or traffic from places you don't serve. Fix: send ads to a page about that exact service and area, and check where your visitors come from.

A 15-minute check you can do today

  1. Open your site on your phone with Wi-Fi off. Count the seconds.
  2. Can you tell your trade and cities in the first screen?
  3. Can you call with one tap?
  4. Send yourself a test enquiry. How long until you get a reply?
Questions

Common questions answered.

How do I know if my website is the problem or my marketing is?

Look at visits and enquiries separately. Few visits means people aren't finding you. Plenty of visits but few enquiries means the site isn't convincing them. Enquiries that don't turn into jobs points to follow-up.

Do I need a whole new website?

Not always. Fixing speed, contact options and photos can help a lot. If the site is slow, dated and hard to update, a new one is usually faster than patching.
