How contractors show up in ChatGPT and other AI answers.

More homeowners now ask an assistant who to hire. Here is what actually raises your odds of being named, in plain words.

Updated December 2026 · 7 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

Nobody can buy or guarantee a spot in an AI answer. What raises your odds:

  • A website that says plainly what you build, where, and how to reach you.
  • Your business mentioned on other sites the assistants already trust: directories, review sites, trade groups, local news.
  • Steady, specific reviews.
  • Being indexed by both Google and Bing.
  • Pages that answer the questions homeowners ask, like cost and timelines.

How do AI assistants decide which contractors to name?

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers read the web and summarize what they find. When a homeowner asks for "the best pool builder near me", the assistant pulls from pages it can reach and trusts, then names the businesses that show up clearly and often.

Most of that evidence lives on other people's sites. One 2026 analysis of 25 million links cited by AI assistants (Muck Rack) found about 84% pointed to earned coverage, not a company's own pages. Your website matters, but mentions elsewhere matter more.

The answers also move around. A 2026 SparkToro study ran the same prompts thousands of times and found ChatGPT and Google's AI answers returned the same list of brands less than 1% of the time. Treat this as raising your odds, not winning a ranking.

Step 1: Make your website easy to read

Assistants can only repeat what they can understand. On your site, say these things in plain text, not inside images:

  • Your business name, your trade and the cities you serve, near the top of the home page.
  • A list of the services you offer, and the ones you don't.
  • Your phone number and a way to reach you on every page.
  • Real project photos with captions: the city, the scope, the year.
  • Fast loading on phones.

Step 2: Get indexed by Google and Bing

Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools and submit your sitemap to both. Google's AI answers draw on Google's index. ChatGPT's search has been widely reported to lean on Bing, and Bing is easy to forget.

Claim and complete your Google Business Profile: categories, service area, hours, photos and a short description that names your trade and cities.

Step 3: Get mentioned on other sites

This is the step most contractors skip, and it carries the most weight. Places worth being listed or mentioned:

  • Review and directory sites homeowners use: Google, Yelp, Houzz, Angi, the Better Business Bureau.
  • Your trade association: for pools, the Pool & Hot Tub Alliance; for landscaping, the National Association of Landscape Professionals.
  • Dealer locators from the brands you install, and supplier "find a pro" pages.
  • Your local chamber of commerce, home shows and parade-of-homes listings.
  • Local news and neighborhood sites, for a notable project or community work.

Keep your business name, phone and address the same everywhere. Mismatched details make it harder for anyone, person or machine, to be sure it's you.

Step 4: Earn specific reviews

A review that says "They built our pool in Henderson in eight weeks and handled the HOA" tells an assistant what you do and where. Ask every finished customer, and make it one tap. Our guide to getting more reviews covers how.

Step 5: Answer real questions on your site

Homeowners ask assistants questions like "how much does a pool cost in Phoenix?" or "how long does a patio take?" Pages that answer those questions directly, in the first sentence or two, are easy to quote. A cost page, a process page and a short FAQ go a long way.

Step 6: Check how you show up, every month

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the questions your customers ask, with your city in them. Note who gets named and which sites get cited, then work on being mentioned on those sites. Expect the answers to change from day to day.

What doesn't work

  • Guarantees. Anyone promising you'll be recommended by an AI is promising something they can't control.
  • Fake or paid reviews. The FTC bans fake reviews, and Google removes them.
  • Stuffing keywords or making near-identical pages for dozens of cities. It reads badly to people and to search engines.
  • Counting on an llms.txt file. A 2026 SE Ranking study of 129,000 domains didn't find that it moved citations. Mentions on other sites did.

Questions

Common questions answered.

Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

Not in the regular answers. Where an assistant shows ads, they're labelled as ads. The unpaid answers come from what the assistant finds and trusts on the web.

How long does it take to start showing up in AI answers?

There's no set timeline. Getting indexed takes days to weeks. Building mentions and reviews takes months. Check monthly and look for steady progress rather than a single result.

Do I need a new website to show up in AI answers?

Not always. If your site loads fast on phones and states your trade, cities and services in plain text, start with steps 2 to 4. If it's slow, image-heavy or out of date, a new site makes everything else easier.

Does Google's AI use the same results as ChatGPT?

No. Each assistant uses different sources and they rarely return the same list. Being indexed by both Google and Bing and mentioned across several trusted sites covers more of them.
