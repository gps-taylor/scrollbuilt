Guide · Landscapers

Landscaping website ideas that win jobs.

Design-build clients and maintenance clients want different things. A good landscaping site serves both without mixing them up.

Updated December 2026 · 6 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

Strong landscaping websites separate design-build from maintenance, show before-and-after projects with captions, use dusk and lighting photos, explain the design process, list the service area, and make the next step obvious for each kind of client.

1. Separate design-build from maintenance

A homeowner planning a $40,000 backyard and a property manager looking for weekly service need different pages, photos and calls to action. Give each its own path from the home page.

2. Show before and after

Landscaping is transformation. Pair a bare or overgrown yard with the finished result, and caption it: city, scope, size.

3. Use dusk and lighting photos

Path lights, a fire pit and warm windows at dusk sell the outdoor life homeowners are picturing. If you install lighting, this is your best image.

4. Explain the design process

Consultation, design, proposal, install, walkthrough. Rough timelines for each.

5. Show materials and plants you work with

Pavers, stone, turf, native plants. It helps homeowners name what they want, and helps you get found for it.

6. List your service area

Cities and neighborhoods in plain text. Maintenance clients especially need to know you cover their street.

7. Make maintenance easy to start

A short form for maintenance quotes: address, property size, frequency. Keep design-build enquiries on their own form.

8. Plan for the seasons

Spring planting, summer irrigation, fall cleanup and planning next year's project. Update your home page message with the season.

9. Put reviews next to the button

Two or three specific reviews beside your contact form do more than a reviews page nobody clicks.

Questions

Common questions answered.

What pages should a landscaping website have?

Home, design-build, maintenance, projects, process, service area, reviews and contact. Add a page for each main service you want to be found for, like hardscaping or lighting.

Should landscapers show prices?

Ranges for common projects help. For maintenance, a starting price per visit or month sets expectations.
