How much does a contractor website cost in 2026?

Anywhere from $16 a month to $8,000 a month. Here's what you get at each level, and the costs that don't show up on the price tag.

Updated December 2026 · 6 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

A contractor website in 2026 usually costs one of four ways:

  • Do it yourself (Wix, Squarespace): about $16 to $159 a month, plus your time.
  • Included with job software (Jobber): a basic site from about $49 a month.
  • Done-for-you subscription: from $249 a month billed yearly to about $450 a month.
  • Marketing agency: often $3,500 to $8,000 a month, plus $12,000 to $25,000 to build.

Do-it-yourself builders

Wix runs from $17 to $159 a month on yearly billing, with a free plan; Squarespace starts around $16 a month on yearly billing. They're cheap and flexible. The catch is that you do the design, the writing, the photos and every update, and there's no lead follow-up built in. For a busy owner, the real cost is the evenings.

A site included with job software

Field service software like Jobber includes a basic website on its Core plan, which starts at $49 a month with no commitment. It's handy if you already run jobs there. The sites are simple templates, built to take bookings rather than to sell a $60,000 project.

Done-for-you subscriptions

A company builds and hosts the site for a monthly fee. Hibu, for example, is listed on Capterra at $449 a month. ScrollBuilt is $249 a month billed yearly, or $299 month to month, with no setup fee. Ask what's included beyond the site: lead replies, reminders, review requests, changes.

Marketing agencies

Agencies design, write and run your marketing, often including paid ads. Expect $3,500 to $8,000 a month and $12,000 to $25,000 to build a custom site, often on a 12-month contract. Worth it when you have the budget and want ads managed for you.

Costs that don't show up on the price tag

  • Your time. Ten hours of evenings has a price.
  • Setup and onboarding fees. Ask before you sign.
  • Contract length. A 12-month term at $500 a month is a $6,000 decision.
  • Price jumps at renewal. Intro pricing often ends after year one.
  • Add-ons. Texting, phone numbers, extra pages and changes are often extra.
  • Leaving. Can you take your site, content and domain with you? If not, you pay to rebuild.

How to tell if it's worth it

Work it out from one job. If your average project is $65,000 and your margin is even 20%, one extra job a year is $13,000 of profit against a few thousand dollars of website. The question isn't the monthly price; it's whether the site helps you win more of the leads you already get.

Questions to ask any website company

  1. Who owns the domain? (It should be you.)
  2. Can I download my content, and can I take the whole site if I leave?
  3. How long is the contract, and what does it cost to leave early?
  4. What happens to a new lead the moment it comes in?
  5. What does a change cost, and how fast does it happen?
  6. Can I see what my site would look like before I pay?
Questions

Common questions answered.

What's the cheapest way to get a contractor website?

A do-it-yourself builder on its lowest plan, around $16 to $17 a month on yearly billing, if you're willing to design, write and update it yourself.

Why do agencies charge so much more?

They're selling people's time: design, writing, strategy and often paid ad management. That's worth it if you need those services and have the budget.

How much does ScrollBuilt cost?

$249 a month billed yearly, or $299 month to month, with no setup fee. Your draft is free and you only pay when your site goes live.

Should I pay for a website up front or monthly?

Up-front builds look cheaper over time but leave hosting, changes and updates to you. Monthly plans bundle those in. Compare the three-year total, including your time.
