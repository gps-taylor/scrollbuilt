What makes a good contractor website? 10 things the best ones do.

The best contractor sites sell the work, not the company. Here's what they have in common.

Updated December 2026 · 6 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

Good contractor websites show finished work first, name the trade and city in the headline, make calling one tap, offer one clear next step, explain the process, show real proof, and load fast on a phone.

1. They show the work first

The top of the page is your best finished project, big. Homeowners are buying an outcome, so lead with it.

2. The headline says what and where

"Custom pools in North Scottsdale" beats "Welcome to our website". A visitor should know in one glance that you do what they need, near them.

3. Calling is one tap

Most visitors are on phones. Put a tappable phone number in the header and a short contact form on every page.

4. There's one clear next step

"Get a quote", "Book a design visit" or "Call us". Pick one and repeat it. Three competing buttons is none.

5. Project photos have captions

City, scope and year under each project: "Freeform pool and travertine deck, Henderson, 2026." It helps homeowners picture their own job and helps search engines understand where you work.

6. They explain the process

A short timeline from first call to finished job. Big projects feel risky; showing the steps takes the fear out.

7. Reviews sit next to the contact button

Proof works best right where someone decides. Two or three specific reviews beat a wall of stars.

8. They answer the price question

You don't need a price list. "Most of our projects fall between X and Y" filters out bad fits and builds trust with good ones.

9. They list the service area

Cities, neighborhoods or ZIP codes, in plain text. It answers "do you come to me?" before they have to ask.

10. They're fast

A site that takes five seconds to load on a phone has lost a chunk of its visitors before they see anything. See our speed guide.

What to leave off

  • Stock photos of people shaking hands.
  • Sliders that rotate every three seconds.
  • "Welcome to our website" and long company histories at the top.
  • Auto-playing music or video with sound.
  • Pop-ups the moment the page loads.

Questions

Common questions answered.

How many pages does a contractor website need?

Usually five to eight: home, services, projects, process, about, reviews and contact, plus a page for each main service if you want to be found for it.

Should I put prices on my website?

Ranges help. A line like "most projects fall between X and Y" sets expectations without locking you into a quote.
