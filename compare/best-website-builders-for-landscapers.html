Best website builders for landscapers (2026)

The main ways to get a landscaping website, what each costs, and who each one is right for.

Updated December 2026 · 7 min read · By ScrollBuilt

In short

If you mostly sell design-build projects and want a finished site with follow-up, ScrollBuilt. If maintenance is most of your work and you run jobs in Jobber, its included site may be enough. For agency-managed marketing, a landscape agency or Scorpion. To build it yourself, Wix or Squarespace.

Landscapers sell two very different things: big design-build projects and recurring maintenance. The right website option depends on which pays your bills. Here are the main ways landscapers get a website in 2026.

ScrollBuilt

Best for: Landscapers who want a finished site that shows the work coming to life, plus lead follow-up, without an agency.

Pricing not published.

Best for: Landscape companies that want an agency focused on their industry.

Good: Industry focus and content marketing experience for landscape businesses.

Hibu

Not published; Capterra lists from $449 a month.

Best for: Owners who want one company for a website plus listings and ads.

Good: Done-for-you website with marketing services available.

Watch out for: Reviews report a 12-month contract. Not built specifically for your trade. Read the comparison.

Scorpion

Not published.

Best for: Larger operations with a real ad budget that want an agency.

Good: Full-service agency: websites, SEO, paid ads, video and reporting.

Watch out for: Typically 12-month contracts for marketing technology and SEO (Scorpion FAQ). Read the comparison.

Jobber

Core from $49 a month, no commitment, includes a basic website.

Best for: Owners who already run jobs in Jobber and need a simple site.

Good: Scheduling, quotes, invoicing and payments in one place.

Watch out for: The website is a basic template, built for bookings more than selling big projects. Read the comparison.

Thryv

Varies by package; a full system listed at $403 a month plus onboarding (November 2026).

Best for: Owners who want one system for scheduling, payments and marketing.

Good: Broad all-in-one toolset.

Watch out for: Packages vary and some carry a six-month commitment. Not built specifically for landscapers. Read the comparison.

Wix or Squarespace

Wix $17 to $159 a month (yearly billing); Squarespace from about $16 a month.

Best for: Owners with more time than budget who like building things.

Good: Cheap, flexible, lots of templates.

Watch out for: You do all the design, writing and updates. No lead follow-up. Read the comparison.

GoHighLevel

$97 to $497 a month, plus usage fees.

Best for: Tech-minded owners, or owners whose agency runs it for them.

Good: CRM, funnels, websites, texting and automation in one toolkit.

Watch out for: Built mainly for agencies; someone has to set it up. Read the comparison.

How to choose

  1. Decide who does the work. If you won't build and update it yourself, rule out do-it-yourself builders.
  2. Decide if you need ads run for you. If yes, you're looking at an agency or a bundle. If not, a website subscription costs far less.
  3. Look before you sign. Ask to see your own site, or a real example for your trade, before committing.
  4. Read the contract. Term length, early exit cost, and whether you can take the site with you.
  5. Check the follow-up. Ask what happens to a lead the moment it arrives.

Questions

Common questions answered.

What is the best website builder for landscapers?

For design-build work with lead follow-up and no agency, ScrollBuilt. For mostly maintenance work already run in Jobber, Jobber's included site. For agency-managed marketing, a landscape agency or Scorpion. To build it yourself, Wix or Squarespace.

Is Jobber's website good enough for a landscaping company?

For maintenance-heavy businesses that need online booking, it can be. For design-build projects, where homeowners need to picture the result, a site built to sell usually does more.

How much should a landscaper spend on a website?

From about $16 a month to build it yourself, to a few hundred a month done for you, to several thousand a month for an agency.
